Food safety

Acidified foods and scheduled processes, in plain language

Why many sauces need a process authority before a co-packer will run them, and where founders usually start.

OpenCopackPublished 2026-11-23Updated 2026-11-23

If your product is a salsa, hot sauce, dressing, or similar shelf-stable sauce, there is a good chance it is an acidified food under FDA rules. Plants that pack those products typically want a scheduled process reviewed by a qualified process authority before they will put your recipe on a line.

OpenCopack is not a process authority and this page is not a legal determination of your product. University programs such as the Cornell Food Venture Center, and the Association of Food and Drug Officials’ process authority directory, are the usual starting points. Your state extension can often tell you who serves your region.

When you brief a plant, say whether you already have that letter. It is one of the first questions a careful co-packer will ask. See how to brief a co-packer.

Source for further reading: Cornell Food Venture Center, and AFDO’s Food Processing Authority directory at afdo.org/directories/fpa.

This is general information, not legal, food-safety, or business advice. Confirm process, labeling, and inspection rules with a process authority and the plant.
