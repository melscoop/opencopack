Money and scale

What a co-packer minimum actually means

Updated 2026-11-19

A minimum order, or minimum run, is the smallest batch a plant is willing to schedule. It is usually set by changeover time, kettle or line size, and the cost of cleaning between recipes. It is not a government number, and it is not standard from plant to plant.

The same idea, five different units

In the public sources behind this directory, plants describe minima in several ways. Examples below are as those sources published them, not as a market average:

  • Gallons — common for sauces and dressings, for example “50 gallons” or “100 gallons.”
  • Pounds — common for blends, meats, and some sauces, for example “200 pounds” or “500 pounds.”
  • Units or bottles — “1,000 units,” “5,000 units,” or a number of bottles or cans.
  • A shift or a production day — the plant wants to fill a line for a defined block of time.
  • Annual volume — some plants publish a yearly pound or gallon expectation, which is a different (and often much larger) commitment than a first-run minimum.

When you compare two plants, convert to the same unit if you can, but also ask what is included: ingredients, packaging, yield loss, and whether a test batch can be smaller than the production minimum.

Costing your own recipe first — food, packaging, labor, and overhead — makes it easier to tell whether a 50-gallon or 1,000-unit minimum is something you can fund. ReciPal is one tool that does this kind of recipe costing. OpenCopack may earn a commission if you sign up through this link.

“None,” “negotiable,” and a blank field

Some official listings literally say “None,” “no minimums - order what you need,” or “short runs are welcome.” That is useful language, and on OpenCopack we flag it as small-run language. It is still not a quote.

“Negotiable” and “varies by product” mean you have to ask. A blank field on this site means the source did not publish a minimum. It does not mean the plant has no minimum. Many plants that omit the number are simply not trying to attract very small runs.

How to use the filter on this site

The directory lets you show only plants that have published a minimum, or only plants whose source used startup, small-run, or no-minimum language. Use both. A published number lets you do the arithmetic; the small-run language tells you they at least discuss first batches in public.

Always reconfirm the number before you budget. Official directories go stale, and a plant can raise a minimum when the line is full.
