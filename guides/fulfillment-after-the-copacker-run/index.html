Fulfillment after the co-packer run

Co-packing ends when cases leave the plant. What to plan for storage, ecommerce shipping, and 3PL warehousing.

OpenCopackPublished 2026-11-25Updated 2026-11-25

A co-packer’s job usually ends when finished cases are ready to leave the plant. What happens next is easy to postpone, and expensive if you do. Some plants will hold inventory for a short window; many will not, or will charge for storage. Ask, before the run is scheduled, how long they will keep finished goods, what the pallet or case configuration is, and whether they will ship to a warehouse, a retailer, or only back to you.

If you sell to retail or foodservice, the buyer may take pallets. If you sell direct-to-consumer or through your own ecommerce site, you need a place to store those cases and a way to pick, pack, and ship individual orders. That work is often done by a third-party logistics (3PL) warehouse, not by the co-packer. One option is ShipBob, which stores inventory and ships DTC and ecommerce orders from a fulfillment network. A fulfillment partner is not a co-packer, and signing up for one does not replace a plant quote.

Plan this in parallel with the brief in how to brief a co-packer, especially the timing and pack questions, and with who buys the bottles, pouches, and labels. Having a ship-to address for finished pallets before the first batch is packed avoids a scramble on delivery day.

This is general information, not legal, food-safety, or business advice. Confirm process, labeling, and inspection rules with a process authority and the plant.
