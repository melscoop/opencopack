What “organic” on a listing actually means

A plant page that mentions organic is not the same as a guarantee your recipe can be packed as certified organic.

OpenCopackPublished 2026-11-27Updated 2026-11-27

When an OpenCopack listing shows a certification such as “USDA Organic” or “Organic,” it means the source we cited used that word — for example, a company private-label page that says it has USDA Organic production certification, or a state directory note that a plant is organic certified.

It does not automatically mean:

  • the plant will take your organic recipe on the date you need,
  • every line in the building is organic,
  • or your finished product will be certified without your own organic handling plan and certifier.

If organic is a requirement, say so in the first brief and ask which certifier they work with and whether your run would be on a dedicated or cleaned organic line. We only flag what the source published; we do not audit certificates.

This is general information, not legal, food-safety, or business advice. Confirm process, labeling, and inspection rules with a process authority and the plant.

OpenCopack compiles public lists. It is not a marketplace and does not endorse any plant. Confirm capabilities, minimums, certifications, and capacity directly with the company.
