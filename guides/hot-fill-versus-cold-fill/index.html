Hot-fill versus cold-fill, for founders choosing a plant

A short explanation of two common sauce and beverage packing methods, and why you should not assume a plant does both.

OpenCopackPublished 2026-11-26Updated 2026-11-26

Hot-fill and cold-fill are two different ways of getting a liquid product into a package. In a typical hot-fill, the product is packed while hot so that the heat helps with commercial sterility in the container. Cold-fill packs a cooler product and may rely on other controls (including refrigeration, preservatives, or a different process). The right method depends on the recipe, the pH, the package, and the scheduled process — not on which phrase sounds more artisanal.

Some public plant listings mention hot-fill, cold-fill, or both. Many do not. If our listing does not say, do not infer it from the fact that the plant packs “sauces.” Ask, and be prepared to share process-authority documents.

This page does not tell you which method your product needs. That is a process-authority question.

This is general information, not legal, food-safety, or business advice. Confirm process, labeling, and inspection rules with a process authority and the plant.
